# Positioning: tldrSEC vs. Seeking Alpha

**Date:** 2026-04-17
**Purpose:** Single source of truth for "why this, not Seeking Alpha?" Used in landing FAQ, DM follow-ups, and pricing reviews.

---

## One-line positioning

> Seeking Alpha tells you what other people think about your portfolio. tldrSEC tells you what your portfolio just told the SEC.

---

## When to pick each

Honest framing. Both can coexist.

**Pick Seeking Alpha when:**
- You want analyst opinions and investment theses from 15,000+ contributors
- You read earnings call transcripts as a primary workflow
- You value community discussion and stock idea aggregation

**Pick tldrSEC when:**
- You want to know what a company *actually filed*, not what pundits think about it
- You prefer email over yet another dashboard to log into
- You want Form 4 insider-transaction summaries without paying institutional prices
- You got burned by a paywalled "premium content" trap somewhere else

---

## Where Seeking Alpha wins

Give credit. Don't hedge.

- **Opinion diversity.** 15,000+ contributing analysts is a real moat. If you want five takes on Tesla's quarter, they have them.
- **Earnings call transcripts.** Hard to replicate, well-indexed, searchable.
- **Track record.** They've been around. Retail investors know the name.

---

## Where tldrSEC wins

Three things, with source language from pain-point research:

### 1. Email-native delivery

Most competitors (including Seeking Alpha) push you into a dashboard. tldrSEC ships summaries to your inbox.

> "I want summaries in my inbox, not another app to check."

No login. No tab to remember. The summary is there when the filing drops.

### 2. Filing-focused, not opinion-mixed

Seeking Alpha's 15,000 contributors is also its weakness: signal-to-noise.

> "15,000 authors = too many articles to filter."

tldrSEC only does SEC filings — 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, Form 4, Form 144. If a company files it, we summarize it. If it's a think-piece, we don't touch it.

### 3. No billing traps

Direct quote from Seeking Alpha Reddit feedback (competitive-landscape research):

> "Charged $299 immediately after trial without warning."

Also documented: "Difficult cancellation process, auto-renewals."

tldrSEC: 7-day trial, no card required. Cancel self-serve at any time. No surprise charges.

## Market gap thesis

Why does this product exist at all?

> $10-50/month AI-powered SEC filing summaries delivered via email. Seeking Alpha is $239/yr for opinions; free alerts (SEC.gov, CapEdge) are raw filings with zero analysis. Nothing sits in between at retail price points.

That gap is the wedge.
